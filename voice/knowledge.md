# Heating and cooling basics (FAQ)

## What is a heat pump?
A heat pump moves heat from one place to another instead of creating it. In warm weather it carries heat out of the house. In cool weather it carries heat in. One machine does both jobs.

## What does an air filter do?
The filter catches dust and hair before they reach the system. A clogged filter blocks airflow, makes the system work harder, and can cause the unit to freeze or break down sooner. Many households check theirs every month or two and replace it when it looks grey and clogged.

## Why is my AC running but not cooling?
Common causes are a dirty filter, a thermostat set wrong, ice on the indoor coil, or a problem outside with the condenser unit. Checking the filter and the thermostat is a safe first step. Anything beyond that is a job for a technician.

## What happens on a diagnostic visit?
The technician asks what you noticed, checks the thermostat, filter, indoor unit and outdoor unit, and tests how the system runs. They explain what they found in plain words and give a quote before any repair work starts.

## How can I prepare for a visit?
Clear a path to the indoor unit and the thermostat. Move anything fragile away from the outdoor unit. Make a note of when the problem started, any noises or smells, and anything that changed recently. Secure pets in another room.

## What is a tune-up?
A tune-up is a routine check-over. The technician cleans key parts, checks that the system starts and runs properly, and looks for small problems before they turn into breakdowns.

## What is a thermostat and why does it matter?
The thermostat tells the system when to turn on and off. A wrong setting, a dead battery, or a faulty thermostat can make a healthy system seem broken.

## What is a condensate drain?
Cooling removes moisture from the air, and that water leaves through a small drain pipe. If it clogs, water can back up and may shut the system off or leak. Slime and dirt are the usual cause.

## Why does my system make a noise?
Rattling, buzzing, grinding and squealing can all mean different things, from a loose panel to a worn part. A new or growing noise is a good reason to book a visit. If there is a burning smell or smoke, switch the system off and treat it as an emergency.

## What size system do I need?
It depends on the size and layout of the home, the sunlight it gets, insulation and the number of people living there. A technician measures and calculates this at an estimate visit. A system that is too big or too small works poorly.

## Can I fix it myself?
Changing a filter, checking the thermostat settings and clearing leaves from around the outdoor unit are fine for most homeowners. Electrical parts, refrigerant and gas connections are always for a licensed technician.

## What should I do if I smell gas or see smoke?
Leave the building, do not touch switches, and contact your local emergency services from outside. Do not wait for an appointment.
